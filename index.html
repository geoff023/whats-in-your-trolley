01

Where our food comes from

Australia grows far more food than it eats, yet our shelves still rely on the world. In 2025 we imported food worth US$18.1 billion from 175 countries. Most of it is not fresh produce but packaged goods: sauces, drinks, bakery items and chocolate.

Things to try

Coffee, tea & spices
Brazil and Colombia send raw beans; Switzerland ranks third because of coffee capsules made there.
Cocoa & chocolate
Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia lead, because cocoa is processed there. West Africa grows most of the world's cocoa but barely appears.
Fish & seafood
Despite our long coastline, we import seafood worth US$1 billion a year, led by Vietnam, New Zealand and China.

02

Where we shop

Imported or local, most food reaches us through a supermarket. Woolworths and Coles together run more than one in three of the 5,684 supermarkets mapped by volunteers in OpenStreetMap. Independent grocers fill the gaps, especially outside the big cities.

03

What it costs

Grocery prices have climbed steadily since 2017, with a sharp jump in 2022–23. The charts use the Australian Bureau of Statistics price index, which tracks the price of the same basket of goods over time. A value of +40% means the basket costs 40% more than it did in September 2017.

04

What is inside the pack

We looked at 6,608 products with an Australian barcode in the free Open Food Facts database and grouped them into 17 supermarket aisles. Values are per 100 g (or 100 ml for drinks), so products of any size can be compared.

Median = the middle product: half the products in an aisle have more, half have less. It is not thrown off by a few extreme products.

05

Own brands or name brands?

Supermarkets increasingly sell their own labels next to familiar name brands. Are they healthier, or just cheaper?

Nutri-Score is a front-of-pack grade from A (healthiest) to E (least healthy), widely used in Europe. It works like Australia's Health Star Rating and is calculated from similar nutrients.
